# The determinism matrix, thinkpad, 2026-08-27

**Verdict: 3,010 of 3,010 sessions reproduced.** Zero divergences, zero
errors. 43,620 turns compared, 348,960 field comparisons, **94 complete
sweeps** of the matrix and a 95th cut short by the clock at two sessions,
over roughly seven hours.

The counts around that figure are the deposit's and were re-checked against
it: 94 x 32 + 2 is the 3,010 sessions, the depth table below is 94 x 8 with
the last sweep's two depth-2 sessions added, and the character table is
94 x 4 x {3,2,3} with the same two, both of them confident prompts, which is
the sweep order.

The raw record is deposited at
`/mnt/bulk-store/weaver-testing/determinism-matrix-2026-08-27/`:
`matrix.jsonl` one line per session, `matrix.log`, `summary.json`, and the
artifact's sha256.

## What was asked

The cross-precision confirmation of 2026-08-25 established the protocol
once, on two turns, both of them confident low-entropy tasks. This asked the
prior question on the easiest target available: **with the smallest artifact
and a single precision, does the instrument hold across conditions?** A cell
that fails here is the apparatus failing rather than the model being
unfaithful, which is the whole reason to run it small before running it
large.

## The matrix

Three axes, chosen for what could plausibly break a replay rather than for
coverage's own sake. Repetition alone exercises kernel nondeterminism and
the allocator and nothing else.

- **Prompt character**, eight prompts spanning the draw's confidence. A
  confident answer draws far from a tie, and an open-ended one draws near it,
  and a near-tie is where a float wobble flips the draw and diverges the
  emission. The 2026-08-25 pair sat entirely at the confident end.
- **Depth**, at 2, 8, 16, and 32. A turn at position 2 meets a nearly empty
  resident sequence and one at 32 meets an accumulated one, and the derived
  seed carries the turn ordinal, so depth moves the seed as well as the
  state.
- **Length**, short and long generations, crossing kernel tile boundaries.

Each session serves its turns, unloads fully, reloads, reissues every turn
byte-exact **from the record rather than from the script's constants**, and
compares the eight fields of the cross-precision harness.

**The high-entropy column is the one worth reading.** Probe mean token
entropy ranged 0.149 to 7.411 with a peak per-token entropy of 8.443, so
replay held bit-exact across a wide entropy range and including turns of
very high entropy.

**That is not quite the near-tie claim, and the distinction is worth
keeping.** What flips a draw under a float wobble is the margin between the
top two candidates, and entropy describes the whole distribution rather than
that margin: a long tail carries high entropy while the top two sit far
apart, and such a turn was never near a tie. A peak of 8.443 is also two
orders of magnitude from flat against a vocabulary near 152,000. The
prompts were chosen to draw nearer to ties and the entropy range shows they
spanned the intended ground, but the margin itself is unmeasured here.
`model.field` is the kind that would carry it, and a run electing it could
make the near-tie claim properly.

## What the run also measured

**Decode is 301.4 tok/s sustained**, from a regression of per-turn wall time
on tokens generated across all 43,620 turns: 8.48 ms fixed per turn plus
3.318 ms per token, R^2 0.9985. The per-turn rate rises with generation
length and converges on the same figure - 90.9 tok/s at 1-20 tokens, 301.9
at 400 and above - because the fixed cost is over seventy per cent of a
ten-token turn and negligible in a long one. A median over short turns
understates the decode rate badly, and 96.3 per cent of turns generate
twenty tokens or fewer.

**Prefill is 8,300 to 21,000 tok/s**, 27 to 70 times decode over that range,
at about 2.4 ms for a small block. **The measurement is weak** and is
recorded as such: nearly every turn fed about 20 input tokens, so there is
almost no
variance to fit and the prefill curve at realistic context lengths is
unmeasured here.

**The prefill and cache figures come from the agent's trace and not from
this deposit**, which is stated because the deposit is named above as the
raw record and a reader would go there. A session record carries the turn,
its entropy summary, and the two wall times, and carries neither input token
counts nor resident counts, because `run_session` does not record them.
These figures were read from `karl`'s own trace, whose `model.measurement`
carries `prefill_ns`, `decode_ns`, and the token identifiers. The next run
should record both counts per turn so its deposit stands alone.

**Replay is slower by 0.15 ms and the difference is real.** Source and replay
turns have the same median wall time to the millisecond, and paired over the
43,620 turns the replay side runs +0.154 ms slower with a paired standard
deviation of 4.85 ms, which at this sample is about seven standard errors.
**A dispersion does not bound a difference of means** and an earlier wording
of this clause read it as though it did, calling the difference
unmeasurable. It is measurable and it is negligible in size, one half of one
per cent of a 31 ms turn, and those are different claims. Individual turns
are not identical and should not be: determinism is a property of the
values and not of the clock, which is why `wall_ms` is absent from the
compared fields. Had timing been
compared, every replay would have reported as diverged.

**The KV cache accumulates as chartered.** Input tokens per turn stay near
20, the delta alone, while the resident count climbs monotonically across a
session - a sampled deep run ran 55 tokens on its first turn and about 20 on
each turn after, with the resident count going 100, 122, 144 and on to 837.
A re-prefill would have shown its last turn feeding some 800 tokens rather
than 26.

## Two faults this run exposed in the harness

Both are recorded because the run's value includes what it broke.

**The trace read scaled with history.** `read_runs` parsed the whole
append-only trace to answer a question about its end, twice per session, so
the run slowed 3.6 times as it went - 3.7 s per session at the start and
13.2 s at the end, with 7.9 s of that pure parse against a 251 MiB file. The
run degraded its own instrument by producing evidence. Fixed by reading the
tail: measured 2,265 times faster and verified event-identical against the
full scan on every complete run.

**The sink write races the close.** Fixing the first fault exposed the
second. The trace sink writes after the gate's close answers, so a read
taken the instant a turn closes can miss that turn's events - and the slow
full scan had been acting as an accidental sleep that hid it. With the tail
read the race surfaced immediately and always in the same shape: a run one
turn short, the missing turn always the last. Now waited for explicitly,
which is what the harness meant to do all along.

**Neither fault touches this result.** Both are wall-clock and read-timing
faults in the harness. `read_runs` returned the same index either way, and
the overnight run's slow reads were, if anything, over-waiting for the sink.

## What this does not establish

**Conditions were uniform.** A tight automation loop holds the GPU boosted
and thermally steady, and turns fired back to back with no think time. If
bit-exactness ever depended on clock state, this design would hide it. The
one piece of accidental evidence is reassuring - inter-session gaps grew
from near zero to about eight seconds as the parse slowed, and the results
held throughout - but that was not deliberate and a real test would vary
idle time on purpose.

**This is the 0.5b.** Small models draw nearer to ties than large ones, so
these near-tie cells are plausibly harsher than an 8B's. A clean result here
does not transfer, and the precision ladder owes its own verdict.

**Time to first token is not measured, and is not recorded.** Every
post-request event of a turn is authored in one batch at completion,
verified across 32 of 32 turns sampled, so no first-token instant reaches
the record. TTFT is derivable from `prefill_ns` plus one decode step and
cannot include queueing. **That is what the implementation does rather than
what the charter forbids** - the trace has no member for the instant, which
is a gap to fill and not a prohibition. Filed as its own item.
